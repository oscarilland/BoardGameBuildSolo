# Overview

Players must get Belfast to net zero by 2050 while building their own legacy. The city succeeds or fails together. If it succeeds, money and Green Points both count towards the winner. If it fails, money is worthless and only what you did for the city counts.

- Audience: secondary pupils aged 13–16 (confirmed by the team)

- Players: 4–6, one character each; the first player is chosen at random

- Length: 12 rounds of 2 years each, one turn per player per round. Round 1 is 2026 and round 12 is 2048; when the year reaches 2050 the game ends. The year is shown at the top of the screen.

- Setting: a board made of real Belfast districts

All numbers below were tuned with a computer simulation, including players who try to exploit the rules (see Balance testing). Check them again after the first playtest.

# Resources and scoring

- **Money (£M):** used to buy, convert, co-fund, lobby and upgrade Resilience, and to pay for disasters.

- **Green Points (GP):** the player’s environmental contribution. GP can go down as well as up.

- **Carbon tax:** at the end of every round, each polluting square costs its owner 1 GP.

- **Interim targets:** in the 2030 and 2040 rounds the Climate Meter is checked against a target (see Climate Meter). If the city misses, every polluting square costs its owner 2 GP.

- **Legacy Score** decides the winner, and is worked out differently depending on whether Belfast reaches net zero (see End of game).

# Characters

Starting money and GP differ, and so do the abilities. Starting Legacy Scores are not equal, because money buys squares early and so is worth more than its face value. Instead, the characters are balanced so that each wins a fair share of games.

| **Character**      | **Money** | **GP** | **Ability**                                                                                                   |
|--------------------|-----------|--------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Industrialist      | £40M      | 8      | **Just transition:** when they convert a polluting square, it earns an extra £2M per round as a Green Project |
| Tech Entrepreneur  | £36M      | 10     | Green Projects cost £3M less to buy                                                                           |
| Council Planner    | £38M      | 12     | Each Resilience level costs £2M less                                                                          |
| Farmer             | £36M      | 13     | Nature projects (peatland, woodland) earn +1 GP per round                                                     |
| Student Commuter   | £34M      | 16     | Glider travel is free, and each Glider ride earns +1 GP                                                       |
| Community Activist | £32M      | 18     | Lobbying is free, and a lobbied square also adds nothing to the Climate Meter that round                      |

# Board and squares

The board is a loop of 36 squares through about 6 Belfast districts, with junctions where players choose which way to go. Industry clusters around the Harbour and Titanic Quarter, nature projects around Cave Hill and the Lagan Valley, and housing retrofits in East, West, North and South Belfast.

Squares: 10 Polluting, 12 Green Project (4 of them nature projects), 4 Resilience, 3 Glider Stops and 7 Climate Cards.

| **Square**                                        | **Cost**                  | **Effect each round**                                      |
|---------------------------------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Polluting (gas plant, airport expansion)          | £4–7M                     | +£7M, carbon tax −1 GP, Climate Meter +2                   |
| Green Project (offshore wind, retrofit, peatland) | £8–12M                    | +£2M, +2 GP, Climate Meter −1                              |
| Resilience (flood defences, green roofs)          | £3M / £5M / £7M per level | Your own insurance against disaster costs (see Resilience) |
| Glider Stop                                       | £1M fare                  | Move to any other Glider Stop                              |
| Climate Card                                      | Free                      | Draw a quiz, choice or event card (see Learning)           |

- **Owned squares** cannot be bought. If you land on another player’s Green Project you can co-fund it; on their polluting square you can lobby it.

- **Converting:** an owner can turn a polluting square into a Green Project for £5M. You can make one conversion per turn.

- **Co-funding:** on another player’s Green Project you may pay £3M to the bank. You both gain +1 GP per round. You can only co-fund one project at a time; starting a new deal ends your old one. A deal pays nothing in a round when the square is closed by a Storm, and carries on if the owner goes bankrupt. Polluting squares cannot be co-funded.

- **Lobbying:** on another player’s polluting square you may pay £2M so it earns no money at the end of this round.

## Resilience

Resilience is a personal upgrade. When you land on a Resilience square you may buy your next level. It cuts the money you pay for disasters; it does not stop lost income, closed squares or Droughts.

| **Your level** | **Cost of this level** | **Cut to your disaster costs** |
|----------------|------------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1              | £3M                    | 25%                            |
| 2              | £5M                    | 50%                            |
| 3              | £7M                    | 75%                            |

# Turns and rounds

On your turn:

1.  Roll the dice and move, choosing a direction at junctions.

2.  Act on the square you land on: buy, convert, co-fund, lobby, buy a Resilience level or draw a Climate Card.

3.  If you did not convert in step 2, you may convert one square you own anywhere on the board.

At the end of each round (after every player has had their turn):

1.  Everyone collects money and GP from their squares and co-funding deals.

2.  Carbon tax: each polluting square costs its owner 1 GP.

3.  The Climate Meter updates.

4.  Interim target check (2030 and 2040 rounds only).

5.  Disaster check. Disaster costs are paid straight away; lost income and closed squares apply next round.

6.  The year advances by 2.

# Bankruptcy

If a payment you must make (a disaster or a Climate Card) would take you below £0, your money drops to £0, the rest is written off and you are **bankrupt**.

- While bankrupt you do not move or act on your turn. Your squares still earn GP and still pay the carbon tax, but they earn **no money**.

- At the start of each of your turns, roll one die. On a 6 you are back in the game. After 2 rounds you are back in automatically.

- When you return you receive a £10M loan, and must repay **£15M** when the game ends.

- You can only buy things you can afford, so bankruptcy only comes from payments you cannot avoid.

# Climate Meter and disasters

The Climate Meter is shared by the whole city. It starts at 50. Each round it rises by 3 for the city’s existing emissions, rises by 2 for each owned polluting square, and falls by 1 for each owned Green Project. Polluting moves the meter faster than green projects can bring it down. Disasters hit everyone, whoever caused them.

Belfast reaches net zero if the meter is at 0 or below when the game ends.

## Interim targets

Northern Ireland’s Climate Change Act 2022 sets a cut of at least 48% by 2030 on the way to net zero by 2050. The game checks the meter on the way:

| **Round** | **Target**                                      | **If missed**                               |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| 2030      | Meter at 26 or below (a 48% cut from the start) | Every polluting square costs its owner 2 GP |
| 2040      | Meter at 11 or below                            | Every polluting square costs its owner 2 GP |

## Disasters

Each round, roll one die. A disaster happens if the roll is within the band for the current meter level. The band number also sets how much the disaster costs, so disasters get worse as the city pollutes.

| **Climate Meter** | **Danger band** | **Disaster on a roll of** |
|-------------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| 10 or below       | 1               | 1                         |
| 11–25             | 2               | 1–2                       |
| 26–40             | 3               | 1–3                       |
| 41+               | 4               | 1–4                       |

| **Disaster** | **Effect**                                                                                                                                        |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Flood        | A random district with owned squares floods. Its squares earn nothing next round, and each owner pays £1M × band for every square they own there. |
| Storm        | A random owned square closes for next round. Its owner must pay £2M × band for repairs.                                                           |
| Heatwave     | Every player pays £1M × band in health and cooling costs.                                                                                         |
| Drought      | Nature projects earn no GP next round.                                                                                                            |

Every disaster shows a one-line explanation of how it links to climate change.

# End of game

The game ends when the year reaches 2050, after round 12.

- **Belfast reaches net zero:** Legacy Score = GP + (money − loan repayments) ÷ 5. The highest Legacy Score wins.

- **Belfast misses net zero:** money is worth nothing. Each player loses 5 GP for every polluting square they still own, and their Legacy Score is their GP. The highest score wins, and the end screen states that Belfast missed its target.

- **Ties** are won by whichever tied player owns more Green Projects.

# Learning

- **Quiz cards:** a correct answer earns +2 GP.

- **Choice cards** offer a trade-off. Example: “A gas supplier offers you a cheap contract. Take £6M, and the Climate Meter rises by 2; or pay £2M to retrofit your offices and gain 1 GP.”

- **Event cards** gain or cost money and carry a short real-world fact, for example that Northern Ireland’s Climate Change Act 2022 sets a 2050 net zero target.

- **Interim targets** show that climate targets have deadlines along the way, not just at the end.

- **Disasters** each explain how that disaster links to climate change.

- **End-of-game report:** shows the Climate Meter from 2026 to 2050, each player’s share of emissions, and the decisions that moved the meter most.

# Balance testing

A computer simulation played thousands of games with 4–6 computer players. Some leaned green or polluting at random; others followed fixed strategies to try to exploit the rules. These are simulated results, not playtest results.

| **Players’ average play** | **City reaches net zero** |
|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Mostly polluting          | 13% of games              |
| Mixed                     | 54% of games              |
| Mostly green              | 73% of games              |

| **Test**                                        | **Win rate (fair share 21%)** |
|-------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Pollute as much as possible, convert at the end | 13%                           |
| Only ever go green                              | 17%                           |
| Council Planner who always buys Resilience      | 20%                           |

- Characters: Industrialist 19%, Tech Entrepreneur 17%, Council Planner 19%, Farmer 24%, Student Commuter 20%, Community Activist 21%. The Farmer is slightly strong.

- Players own about 3 squares each by the end, and about 6 in 10 turns have something happen.

- **Two turns per round was tested and rejected.** Players owned more squares (about 5 each), but fewer turns had anything happen, the characters were less balanced, and the game would take twice as long.

# Changes from v3

Kept as evidence of the design options considered, for the SUB1a report.

| **Area**          | **v3**                                      | **v4**                                            | **Why**                                                 |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Pollution penalty | GP lost only if polluting outnumbered green | Carbon tax of 1 GP per polluting square per round | Owning one of each avoided the penalty entirely         |
| Failing net zero  | −3 GP per polluting square                  | Money worthless; −5 GP per polluting square       | “Pollute, then convert at the end” won 1 game in 3      |
| Climate Meter     | Start 45, polluting +1, green −1            | Start 50, polluting +2, green −1                  | Green and polluting play gave almost the same result    |
| Green Projects    | GP only                                     | +£2M per round as well as GP                      | Green players ran out of money                          |
| Co-funding        | Green or polluting squares                  | Green Projects only                               | Two players could profit from pollution with no penalty |
| Resilience        | Shared, per district                        | Personal insurance against disaster costs         | Rarely useful, and protected rivals too                 |
| Disasters         | Fixed costs                                 | Costs grow with the danger band                   | Too cheap to notice                                     |
| Bankruptcy        | Income continued; £10M loan repaid at £10M  | No money while bankrupt; repay £15M               | Was closer to a reward than a punishment                |
| Board             | 50 squares                                  | 36 squares                                        | Too many empty turns                                    |
| Learning          | Facts on cards, quiz +1 GP                  | Interim targets, choice cards, quiz +2 GP         | Learning was attached, not built in                     |
| Carbon tax        | Paid if GP stayed below 5                   | Replaced by the per-square carbon tax             | Taxed your total GP, not your emissions                 |
